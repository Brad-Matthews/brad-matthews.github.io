Brad Matthews

Fintech founder & product leader · Denver

I founded and ran a fintech that sold savings and wealth technology to banks, credit unions and trust companies — through a Series A, a growth round, and an acquisition by a public company. I'm now building the next one.

I started Trizic and led it as CEO. It became Harvest Savings & Wealth Technologies — automated goals-based savings, micro-savings, digital account opening, and a robo-advisory platform wired into Pershing, Apex Clearing and TD Ameritrade. We raised a Series A led by Sorenson Ventures with FIS participating, added a growth round the following year, and in 2021 the company was acquired by Envestnet (NYSE: ENV).

At Apex Fintech Solutions I was Director of Enterprise Sales, advising everyone from early-stage startups to large global institutions on integrating investing and brokerage services through Apex's APIs. At Wala — Techstars 2019, angel-funded — a consumer-friendly credit product. Today I advise Mint Credit. Earlier, institutional investment management at J.P. Morgan, Citi, Barclays and Bear Stearns: the background that taught me how the money actually moves.

What I'm building now is AI-native financial guidance — conversational products that answer real money questions from governed, auditable sources instead of model guesswork. Same customer I've always built for: the institution that has to defend the answer.

How I work

Product first
I'm not an engineer by training — I'm the person who decides what gets built, for whom, and what it has to prove before it ships. Two decades around fintech product means I know which parts are genuinely hard and which just look hard.
AI changed my range
Modern AI development tooling means I now go from spec to running, tested software myself — architecture, implementation and review in one loop. Ideas that used to need a funded team and two quarters get to a working prototype in days. I rebuild my toolchain as the models shift rather than settling into last year's stack.
Where I'm fluent
Bank data aggregation and transaction enrichment, recurring-payment and subscription detection, cash-flow forecasting, brokerage and custody integration, retrieval over curated knowledge corpora, multi-tenant platforms. The hard parts live in the seams — between the aggregator and the ledger, between the model and the disclosure.
Built to survive review
I've sat across the table from bank and broker-dealer diligence, so I build for it from the start: tenant isolation, audit trails, fail-closed behaviour, and provenance on every fact an AI surfaces. If a product can't show which source supported an answer, it isn't finished.
