Brad Matthews

Fintech founder & product leader · Denver

I founded and ran a fintech that sold savings and wealth technology to banks, credit unions and trust companies — through a Series A, a growth round, and an acquisition by a public company. I'm now building the next one.

I started Trizic and led it as CEO. It became Harvest Savings & Wealth Technologies — automated goals-based savings, micro-savings, digital account opening, and a robo-advisory platform wired into Pershing, Apex Clearing and TD Ameritrade. We raised a Series A led by Sorenson Ventures with FIS participating, added a growth round the following year, and in 2021 the company was acquired by Envestnet (NYSE: ENV).

Since then: product work at Apex Fintech Solutions, emerging-markets financial access at Wala (Barclays Accelerator / Techstars), and advising Mint Credit. Earlier, institutional investment management at J.P. Morgan, Citi, Barclays and Bear Stearns — the background that taught me how the money actually moves.

Today I'm building AI-native financial guidance: conversational products that answer real money questions from governed, auditable sources instead of model guesswork. Same customer I've always built for — the institution that has to defend the answer — with a technology stack that finally makes the hard version possible.

How I build now

AI as leverage
AI-assisted development treated as infrastructure, not novelty. Architecture, implementation, review and test coverage in one loop — I cover ground that used to require a team, and I rebuild my toolchain as the models shift rather than settling into last year's stack.
Full stack, literally
Bank data aggregation and transaction enrichment, recurring-payment and subscription detection, cash-flow forecasting, retrieval over governed corpora, multi-tenant platform. The hard parts live in the seams — between the aggregator and the ledger, between the model and the disclosure.
Built to survive review
Single-authority architecture, additive migrations behind flags, fail-closed error handling, tenant isolation, PII tokenization, provenance on every fact an AI surfaces. Governance from the first commit, not retrofitted for a security questionnaire.
Ship, then prove it
Speed is the point, but only if what ships holds up. If a system can't show which source supported an answer, it isn't finished.
